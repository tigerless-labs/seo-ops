# T5 · title / description (per page)

> Downstream: [C11 title/description unique and bounded](../../checklist/references/C11.md) — judges uniqueness + length; either can go red.

## Introduction

These two lines are the page's **appearance in search results**: the title is the blue link,
the description is the gray text under it. They don't affect the page's content — only
whether the person who sees them clicks.

C11 judges two things: **length** (title <=60, desc <=150 characters) and **uniqueness** (no
two pages on the site may collide). Uniqueness fails most often on bulk pages — one template,
hundreds of pages with the identical desc, and the engine concludes these pages carry no
individual value; indexing and ranking get discounted together.

## How to deliver

Two lines at the top of the page doc, written as finished copy (not an outline):

```yaml
title: How to pick F1 student insurance | Acme
description: Walks the school waiver requirements one by one — what insurance to buy during F1, common denial scenarios, and when to switch plans.
```

Suggested site-level formula: `{page topic} | {brand}`, topic first — overlong results are
truncated from the **tail**; losing the brand at the end costs nothing, losing the topic
kills the result.

### Chinese pages: watch the character count

**60 / 150 are character counts, not word counts.** A Chinese character counts as one
character, so a Chinese title can run to 60 hanzi — the checker won't go red. But search
results truncate by **display width**, and one hanzi is roughly two Latin characters wide;
in practice the display runs out around 30 hanzi.

So the working guidance for Chinese pages is **title <=30 chars, desc <=75 chars**.
The checker enforces the hard cap (to catch the absurd); display fit is on the writer.

## Common mistakes

- **Template-generated duplicates** — pSEO pages are the classic case; every page needs at least one genuinely distinguishing variable in its title
- **Desc as a keyword pile** — "insurance, student insurance, F1 insurance, international student insurance": the engine will discard it and scrape the body instead
- **Title stuffed with brand words** — `Acme | ACME | F1 insurance | Acme Insurance`: the topic gets squeezed out
- **Desc promising what the page doesn't have** — readers click in, find otherwise, and bounce; long term it feeds back into ranking
- **Truncated mid-sentence** — "...fits the following three cases: first" is what the reader sees
