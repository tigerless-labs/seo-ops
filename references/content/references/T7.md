# T7 · H2/H3 outline (per page)

> Downstream: [C17 heading levels don't skip](../../checklist/references/C17.md) (no skipped levels) + **the content-shape requirements of C7/C12** (GEO content shape).

## Introduction

The outline serves two things at once:

1. **The table of contents machines read** — h1 → h2 → h3, level by level, no skips. This is what C17 judges.
2. **Whether AI can lift a single section on its own** — this is the core of GEO.

The second deserves expansion: **AI cites paragraphs, not whole pages.** When ChatGPT answers
a question, it grabs the opening sentences of one section — it doesn't stuff your whole
article into the answer. So whether a section can be lifted depends on whether it **still
stands once out of context**:

- Is the section heading the sentence a reader would actually ask?
- Does the first paragraph state the conclusion outright, or warm up for three sentences first?

"Build up, then conclude" is a human writing habit, but fatal to AI extraction — what gets
extracted is the warm-up.

## How to deliver

The outline is **delivered structured in this page's SEO appendix** (the section at the end of
the page doc, in the same block as title/h1 — not written into the body): the hierarchy plus
the gist of each section's first sentence:

```yaml
h1: How to choose health insurance during your F1 student visa

outline:
  - h2: Do F1 students have to buy health insurance?
    lead: Most schools mandate it and the plan must satisfy the waiver terms; it's a school rule, not federal law.
  - h2: What do school waiver requirements usually check?
    lead: Four things — annual limit, deductible, repatriation/return clause, coverage across the whole term.
    h3:
      - title: What deductible qualifies
        lead: Most schools require ≤$500...
  - h2: When can outside insurance replace the school plan?
    lead: When every waiver term is met and proof is filed before the waiver deadline.
```

`lead` is **the section's conclusion in one sentence**, not finished paragraph copy — when
writing the body, expand it into the first paragraph.

**Why one contiguous section, not scattered into the body**: the outline is an input checked
item by item (C17 judges the levels, C7/C12 judge the content shape); split it across
sections and someone has to dig it out of the middle of copy. As one block, the writer sees
at a glance whether any level skips, and the reviewer doesn't have to read the full text.
Every field in the appendix follows the same logic — including image alt ([T14](T14.md)),
which is keyed by image filename, not by position in the body.

Key points:

- **Only an h2 can have an h3 under it** — no h2 straight to h4, and no h1 straight to h3
- **Guide / FAQ content: h2 in the user's own words, verbatim.** "Do F1 students have to buy
  health insurance?" beats "Overview of insurance requirements" — the former is what someone
  would actually type into a search box
- **Each section's first paragraph is the conclusion**; the argument comes after
- FAQs are written as standalone Q&A blocks (feeding FAQPage JSON-LD, see [C12](../../checklist/references/C12.md))

## Common mistakes

- **Skipped levels** — most often for visual reasons: "h2 renders too big, use h3".
  **Hierarchy is semantics, font size is style**; if you want it smaller, have UI change the CSS, not the tag
- **h2 as a marketing slogan** — "All-round protection": neither a question nor a topic; useless even if AI lifts it
- **Conclusion at the end of the section** — the answer only emerges after reading the whole section; AI lifts the front, and the front is the warm-up
- **Three questions crammed into one section** — the lifted passage can't answer them all; AI skips ahead to someone else's page
- **Outline delivered without the leads** — then what frontend assembles is still a pile of empty headings
