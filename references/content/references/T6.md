# T6 · the one H1 line (per page)

> Downstream: [C17 heading levels don't skip](../../checklist/references/C17.md) — judges "exactly one h1"; more or fewer both go red.

## Introduction

`<h1>` is the page telling machines "this page is about this". **One per page, no more, no fewer.**

It is often conflated with [title](T5.md), but they are two different things:

| | title | h1 |
|---|---|---|
| appears in | search results / browser tab | top of the page body |
| reader | people not yet on the page | people already on it |
| constraints | <=60 chars, usually has to carry the brand | no hard length limit, no brand needed |

So the two are **the same topic but not necessarily the same wording**. The title often has
to compress to fit 60 characters; the h1 can be written more naturally and completely.

## How to deliver

One line at the top of the page doc:

```yaml
h1: How to choose health insurance during your F1 student visa
```

Against the T5 example above, the title is `How to pick F1 student insurance | Acme` —
the same thing, said more completely in the h1, because it reserves no room for the brand.

## Common mistakes

- **Two delivered** — the main heading and the subheading both want to be h1. The subheading is an h2, or not a heading at all (it's a lede)
- **The site name / logo as h1** — the logo belongs to site-wide navigation, not this page's topic; a common leftover from old templates
- **No h1, just one big visual headline** — **big font ≠ h1**. Visual treatment belongs to UI, semantic hierarchy belongs here; however large a `<div class="hero-title">` is, machines can't tell it's a heading
- **h1 as a marketing slogan** — "Protection wherever you go": neither machines nor readers learn what the page is about
- **The same h1 on every page** — usually a template putting the brand slogan into the h1; equivalent to no page declaring its topic
