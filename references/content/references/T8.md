# T8 · language config and bilingual pairs (per page)

> Downstream: [C22 hreflang reciprocal pairs](../../checklist/references/C22.md) — human-review item (reciprocal pairs + x-default).

## Introduction

`hreflang` tells engines "this page exists in other languages, at these URLs". Its judgment
has a counterintuitive property:

**It must point both ways; one missing side voids the whole pair.**

The Chinese page declares "my English version is at /guide/x", and the English page must also
declare "my Chinese version is at /zh/guide/x". Declare only one side and the engine ignores
the whole relationship — not discounted, treated as absent.

The consequence: the two language versions are treated as **duplicates of each other**; the
engine indexes one and demotes the other. The version you labored to translate may never
enter the index at all.

## How to deliver

At the top of the page doc:

```yaml
locales: [en, zh]                # the language versions this page exists in
default_locale: en               # fallback when no language matches (→ x-default)
```

Single-language pages **declare explicitly**, never leave it blank:

```yaml
locales: [en]
locale_note: en only            # explicit: this page is English-only, not "Chinese version pending"
```

The value of that line: **"English only" and "the Chinese version isn't delivered yet" look
exactly the same in the data.** Unless it's written down, nobody knows whether to chase.

**Bilingual pages deliver en/zh together, no one-sided delivery.** If the Chinese version
lands two weeks later, hreflang is broken for those two weeks — either ship both together, or
declare it single-language first and switch to bilingual once the Chinese version is ready.

## Common mistakes

- **The Chinese version ships late** — the most common. The interim isn't "not finished yet", it's **a broken declaration live in production**
- **Body translated, title/description not** — a Chinese page wearing an English title; readers in the search results don't click
- **The two versions don't correspond** — hreflang declares "the same content in different languages"; drop two sections from the Chinese version in passing, and it's no longer a correspondence
- **A region code used as a language code** — `hreflang="cn"` is wrong (`cn` is a country code); Simplified Chinese is `zh-Hans` or `zh-CN`
- **x-default forgotten** — without the fallback, readers whose language isn't listed get assigned at random
